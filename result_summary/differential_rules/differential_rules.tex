\colorbox{soft}{\parbox{0.96\textwidth}{
Each FOV-level pair is attraction ($+1$), avoidance ($-1$), or no significant rule ($0$).
A rule is eligible only when both cell types have at least 20 cells. Net score is
$100(N_{\rm attraction}-N_{\rm avoidance})/N_{\rm eligible}$.
\hypertarget{test-guide}{\textbf{How to read the tests.}}
``Ordered FDR'' asks whether values move consistently from Control to Mild to Severe.
C--M, M--S and C--S compare the named pair of stages. ``Organ FDR'' compares Colon with
Duodenum within one stage. FDR values are corrected for the relevant rule screen; smaller
is stronger, and values below 0.05 are called significant.
Temporal FDR tests the largest biopsy-level net-score gap among the three post-transplant
windows; Control is shown only as context. Pooled temporal FDR jointly corrects all rules
in two broader post-transplant comparisons.
The discovery notebook screens every pair rule and draws a result when its best corrected
test has FDR $\leq0.05$ and its largest stage gap is at least 20 percentage points; no rule
name participates in selection. Rule--abundance plots use the same eligible FOVs for all
three lines. These are spatial associations, not causal mechanisms.

\section{Evidence that the method resolves spatial structure}

The clearest separation between composition and organization is Duodenal
Fibroblast $\rightarrow$ Muscle: under pathological staging the rule becomes much more
avoidant while both cell populations increase, so the blue trajectory cannot be a copy of
cell loss.

\evidence{Strong}

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{summary_downloads/tex_method_fibroblast_muscle_path.pdf}
  \caption{\changevalues{Fibroblast $\rightarrow$ Muscle}{Fibroblast}{Muscle}
  The x-axis reports FOVs with a significant rule / eligible FOVs. Eligibility requires at
  least 20 cells of both types. See the \hyperlink{test-guide}{test guide above}.}
  \label{fig:change-values}
\end{figure}

The same Fibroblast $\rightarrow$ Muscle separation appears when the biopsies are regrouped
by clinical score, which makes it unlikely that the result is created by one severity
definition.

\evidence{Strong}

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{summary_downloads/tex_method_fibroblast_muscle_clinical.pdf}
  \caption{Values follow Figure~\ref{fig:change-values}; stages are defined by clinical score.}
\end{figure}

Direction is also retained rather than collapsing a cell pair into undirected co-occurrence:
Colon CD4T $\rightarrow$ Goblet relaxes strongly with pathological severity, while the
reverse rule starts more avoidant and changes by a different amount.

\evidence{Moderate}

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{summary_downloads/tex_new_colon_cd4_goblet.pdf}
  \caption{Values follow Figure~\ref{fig:change-values}; stages use pathological score in Colon.}
\end{figure}

Reading the reverse direction separately shows why it must remain a separate rule: Goblet
$\rightarrow$ CD4T has its own baseline, trend, corrected tests, and abundance context.

\evidence{Moderate}

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{summary_downloads/tex_method_direction_goblet_cd4.pdf}
  \caption{Values follow Figure~\ref{fig:change-values}; this reverse rule is calculated
  independently from CD4T $\rightarrow$ Goblet.}
\end{figure}

The method also preserves organ context. Goblet $\rightarrow$ Endothelial remains strongly
avoidant in Colon but is much nearer neutral in Duodenum at matched clinical stages, and the
lower panels show that this is not simply the same difference in cell abundance.

\evidence{Strong}

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.94\textwidth]{summary_downloads/tex_method_organ_goblet_endothelial.pdf}
  \caption{\organvalues{Goblet $\rightarrow$ Endothelial}{Goblet}{Endothelial}
  Colon and Duodenum are compared within each clinical stage; organ FDR is corrected jointly
  across all tested rules and stages. Point labels report FOVs with a significant rule /
  eligible FOVs. See the \hyperlink{test-guide}{test guide above}.}
  \label{fig:organ-values}
\end{figure}

\section{Rule-level alignment with the spatial-atlas paper}

The strongest direct alignment is Paneth-cell loss. \paperquote{loss of Paneth cells}
The count panel shows that Paneth eligibility collapses in Severe Duodenum, while the rule
panel shows what remains measurable rather than confusing ineligibility with ``no rule.''

\evidence{Strong for Paneth loss; moderate for the spatial rule}

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.96\textwidth]{summary_downloads/tex_paper_paneth_to_epithelial.pdf}
  \caption{\countstatevalues{Paneth}{Paneth $\rightarrow$ Epithelial}
  Both panels use pathological stages in Duodenum. See the
  \hyperlink{test-guide}{test guide above}.}
  \label{fig:count-state-values}
\end{figure}

The reverse direction adds a spatial hypothesis within the small Paneth-rich remainder:
Epithelial $\rightarrow$ Paneth rises with severity, but only 6 Severe FOVs are eligible, so
the figure supports a selected surviving niche rather than the whole Severe group.

\evidence{Moderate}

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.96\textwidth]{summary_downloads/tex_paper_epithelial_to_paneth.pdf}
  \caption{Values follow Figure~\ref{fig:count-state-values}; the reverse rule is calculated
  independently and ``eligible'' gives the FOV count passing the 20-cell threshold.}
\end{figure}

Endocrine accumulation is recovered just as directly. \paperquote{accumulation of endocrine
cells in the crypts} No Control FOV reaches the eligibility threshold, whereas disease
creates eligible FOVs with a predominantly attractive Endocrine $\rightarrow$ Epithelial
rule; this is a clear qualitative translation, not a Control-to-disease significance test.

\evidence{Moderate}

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.96\textwidth]{summary_downloads/tex_paper_endocrine_context.pdf}
  \caption{Values follow Figure~\ref{fig:count-state-values}; no Control FOV is eligible for
  Endocrine $\rightarrow$ Epithelial.}
\end{figure}

The atlas also reports a ``prominent reduction in immunoglobulin A--secreting plasma cells.''
Colon CD4T $\rightarrow$ Plasma falls from a clearly attractive Control context toward
neutrality in disease, while the abundance lines show how much of that shift accompanies
the changing cell populations.

\evidence{Moderate}

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{summary_downloads/tex_paper_colon_cd4_plasma.pdf}
  \caption{Values follow Figure~\ref{fig:change-values}; stages use clinical score in Colon.}
\end{figure}

CD8T $\rightarrow$ Plasma independently shows the same loss of an attractive Control
context, making the plasma result broader than one T-cell antecedent; the Mild estimate is
nevertheless based on very few eligible FOVs.

\evidence{Moderate-to-weak}

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{summary_downloads/tex_paper_colon_cd8_plasma.pdf}
  \caption{Values follow Figure~\ref{fig:change-values}; stages use clinical score in Colon.}
\end{figure}

The atlas found CD8 enrichment only in a subset of patients. Duodenal CD8T $\rightarrow$
Goblet changes strongly in the pathological grouping, so it is a relevant immune--epithelial
signal, but this FOV-level plot cannot identify or validate the paper's patient subset.

\evidence{Moderate}

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{summary_downloads/tex_paper_duodenum_cd8_goblet.pdf}
  \caption{Values follow Figure~\ref{fig:change-values}; stages use pathological score in
  Duodenum, and these are FOV averages rather than patient-cluster values.}
\end{figure}

The current pairwise analysis does not recover the paper's patient-specific
macrophage/neutrophil immune states, and no post-transplant time rule passes FDR 0.05. Those
paper results are therefore kept outside the alignment claim rather than represented by a
weak substitute.

\section{New biologically interpretable findings}

Duodenal Macrophage $\rightarrow$ Muscle becomes markedly more avoidant with pathological
severity even though neither cell population disappears. This is biologically interesting
because muscularis macrophages are known to participate in gut neuromuscular homeostasis
(\href{https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4149228/}{M\"uller et al., 2014}), but the
present result does not identify that macrophage subtype.

\evidence{Strong data signal; biologically exploratory}

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{summary_downloads/tex_new_macrophage_muscle.pdf}
  \caption{Values follow Figure~\ref{fig:change-values}; stages use pathological score in
  Duodenum.}
\end{figure}

Duodenal CD4T $\rightarrow$ Goblet becomes substantially less avoidant under pathological
staging, while the cell-share trajectories are much smaller than the rule movement; this
suggests altered immune access to the epithelial compartment rather than composition alone.

\evidence{Strong}

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{summary_downloads/tex_new_duodenum_cd4_goblet_path.pdf}
  \caption{Values follow Figure~\ref{fig:change-values}; stages use pathological score in
  Duodenum.}
\end{figure}

The clinical grouping independently preserves the same CD4T $\rightarrow$ Goblet direction,
which strengthens the observation because the two severity definitions assign biopsies
differently.

\evidence{Strong}

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{summary_downloads/tex_new_duodenum_cd4_goblet_clinical.pdf}
  \caption{Values follow Figure~\ref{fig:change-values}; here biopsies are grouped by clinical
  rather than pathological score.}
\end{figure}

Colon Goblet $\rightarrow$ Muscle is strongly avoidant in Control and relaxes in disease;
the matched cell shares do not reproduce the blue trajectory, making this a candidate change
in epithelial--stromal organization.

\evidence{Strong}

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{summary_downloads/tex_method_goblet_muscle_forward.pdf}
  \caption{Values follow Figure~\ref{fig:change-values}; stages use pathological score in Colon.}
\end{figure}

Muscle $\rightarrow$ Goblet changes in the same broad direction but has independently
estimated states and effect sizes, supporting a bidirectional reorganization rather than
using the reverse arrow as a duplicate.

\evidence{Strong}

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{summary_downloads/tex_method_goblet_muscle_reverse.pdf}
  \caption{Values follow Figure~\ref{fig:change-values}; the reverse rule is tested
  independently under pathological staging in Colon.}
\end{figure}

The same Goblet $\rightarrow$ Muscle rule is also organ-specific at matched clinical stages:
Colon is strongly avoidant while Duodenum stays near neutral, and both cell-abundance
profiles are visible below the rule comparison.

\evidence{Strong}

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.94\textwidth]{summary_downloads/tex_new_organ_goblet_muscle.pdf}
  \caption{Values and ``$n$'' follow Figure~\ref{fig:organ-values}; clinical stages are matched
  between organs, and the organ difference passes FDR 0.05 in at least two stages.}
\end{figure}

Fibroblast $\rightarrow$ Goblet shows the opposite organ ordering in disease, becoming more
avoidant in Duodenum than Colon at Mild and Severe; the result is interesting because the
organ gap persists after using rule-specific eligible FOVs for the abundance context.

\evidence{Moderate}

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.94\textwidth]{summary_downloads/tex_new_organ_fibroblast_goblet.pdf}
  \caption{Values and ``$n$'' follow Figure~\ref{fig:organ-values}; clinical stages are matched
  between organs.}
\end{figure}

Colon Goblet $\rightarrow$ SMV is nearly maximally avoidant in Control and Mild but relaxes
in Severe while Goblet and SMV abundance move differently; this makes it a strong numerical
lead for epithelial--vascular reorganization, although the atlas did not state this rule.

\evidence{Strong data signal; biologically exploratory}

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{summary_downloads/tex_new_goblet_smv.pdf}
  \caption{Values follow Figure~\ref{fig:change-values}; stages use pathological score in Colon.}
\end{figure}

Colon Fibroblast $\rightarrow$ Plasma moves from attraction toward neutrality, a plausible
loss of an immune--stromal niche that complements the paper's plasma-cell depletion; it is
kept as a hypothesis because the ordered trend is borderline and the atlas gives no direct
spatial counterpart.

\evidence{Moderate}

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{summary_downloads/tex_new_fibroblast_plasma.pdf}
  \caption{Values follow Figure~\ref{fig:change-values}; stages use pathological score in
  Colon. The Control--Severe contrast passes FDR 0.05,
  whereas ordered-trend FDR is approximately 0.052.}
\end{figure}

The remaining three results are scientifically interesting precisely because the plots make
their limitation visible: each concerns a rare-cell niche and therefore has a very small
eligible endpoint group. Paneth $\rightarrow$ Mast becomes strongly avoidant, but only three
Severe FOVs support the estimate.

\evidence{Exploratory}

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{summary_downloads/tex_new_paneth_mast.pdf}
  \caption{Values and ``rule $n$'' follow Figure~\ref{fig:change-values}; stages use
  pathological score in Duodenum.}
\end{figure}

Paneth $\rightarrow$ Plasma\_CD38 also becomes avoidant and passes corrected comparisons,
which could mark reorganization of the surviving Paneth-rich niche, but only five Severe
FOVs contain enough of both cell types.

\evidence{Exploratory}

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{summary_downloads/tex_new_paneth_plasma_cd38.pdf}
  \caption{Values follow Figure~\ref{fig:change-values}; stages use pathological score in
  Duodenum and only jointly eligible FOVs contribute.}
\end{figure}

Goblet $\rightarrow$ Mesenchymal\_VIM has a large ordered change and therefore deserves
follow-up as an epithelial--mesenchymal hypothesis, but its endpoint estimates use only
three and four eligible FOVs and no individual stage contrast passes FDR 0.05.

\evidence{Exploratory}

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{summary_downloads/tex_new_goblet_mesenchymal_vim.pdf}
  \caption{Values follow Figure~\ref{fig:change-values}; stages use pathological score in
  Duodenum, ordered-trend FDR is 0.027, and eligible FOV counts are shown.}
\end{figure}

\subsection*{Exploratory changes after transplantation}

Although it does not pass correction (temporal FDR 0.335), Colon CD8T $\rightarrow$ Goblet
shifts from a mildly avoidant Control context toward markedly stronger avoidance after
100 days, making it the clearest immune--epithelial temporal lead in Colon.

\evidence{Exploratory; not statistically significant}

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.82\textwidth]{summary_downloads/tex_new_time_colon_cd8_goblet.pdf}
  \caption{Each dot is one biopsy's mean rule state across eligible FOVs, multiplied by 100;
  diamonds are time-group means and eligible $n$ is the contributing biopsy count.
  Temporal FDR tests the largest difference among $<30$, 30--100 and $>100$ days and is
  corrected across all tested Colon pair rules; Control is not part of that test. See the
  \hyperlink{test-guide}{test guide above}.}
  \label{fig:time-values}
\end{figure}

Colon SMV $\rightarrow$ Endothelial loses much of its attractive net score after 100 days;
the trajectory is large and vascularly relevant, but remains exploratory because temporal
FDR is 0.362.

\evidence{Exploratory; not statistically significant}

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.82\textwidth]{summary_downloads/tex_new_time_colon_smv_endothelial.pdf}
  \caption{Values follow Figure~\ref{fig:time-values}; this is a separately tested directional
  Colon rule.}
\end{figure}

Colon SMV $\rightarrow$ Goblet becomes more avoidant again after an early relaxation,
suggesting a possible late epithelial--vascular change; temporal FDR is 0.362, so this is a
follow-up lead rather than confirmed evidence.

\evidence{Exploratory; not statistically significant}

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.82\textwidth]{summary_downloads/tex_new_time_colon_smv_goblet.pdf}
  \caption{Values follow Figure~\ref{fig:time-values}; the same biopsy-level calculation and
  eligibility threshold are used.}
\end{figure}

In Duodenum, Plasma\_CD38 $\rightarrow$ Fibroblast is the closest pooled temporal lead:
the $<30$ versus $\geq30$-day comparison has a 34-percentage-point gap and joint FDR 0.086,
which is suggestive but does not pass 0.05.

\evidence{Exploratory; near-significant but not statistically significant}

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.94\textwidth]{summary_downloads/tex_new_time_duodenum_pooled.pdf}
  \caption{Each point is one directional pair rule. The x-axis is the difference between the
  two named groups in biopsy-level net score, in percentage points; the y-axis is joint FDR.
  The lower panel contains the Plasma\_CD38 $\rightarrow$ Fibroblast result. FDR is corrected
  jointly across both pooled contrasts and every tested Duodenal pair rule.}
\end{figure}
