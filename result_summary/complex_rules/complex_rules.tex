\textbf{How to read the plots.}
A rule is evaluated inside one FOV. Rules with the same cell types and arrow sides are shown
together even if they were mined around different centers: their numeric metrics are averaged
within that FOV, and the direction is kept distinct. The displayed mining FDR, where available,
is a constituent-rule audit value, \emph{not} a newly tested combined-rule FDR.
\emph{Attraction} and \emph{avoidance} are association-rule states
(Lift above or below 1), not physical forces, cell motion, or causality. The analysis contains
598,093 cells in 267 FOVs, 73 biopsies, and 71 patients after the stated exclusion.

Unless a figure says otherwise, an eligible FOV has at least 20 cells of every named type.
Stored occurrences must pass mining FDR $\leq0.05$ and the investigation gates:
support $\geq0.01$ and confidence $\geq0.5$ for attractions, or expected support
$P(A)P(B)=\mathrm{support}-\mathrm{leverage}\geq0.01$ for avoidances. Most automatic discovery
views additionally retain only rules that add information beyond stored shorter rules; the
parent--complex reversal screen deliberately uses all passing occurrences of both rules.

The catalogue prioritizes large visible patterns with useful eligible-FOV and patient counts.
The stage and strength notebooks retain FOV-, biopsy-, and patient-level tests for audit; the
parent-reversal notebook instead shows descriptive counts. The figures omit test labels to keep
the biological pattern readable. Cell-type abundance panels are diagnostic context only, not
findings about cell loss or abundance.

\section{New findings}

Every rule in this section first entered through an automatic screen: enough eligible/rule-bearing
FOVs and patients, followed by a visible prevalence or conditional-Lift gap. Biological
interpretation happened only after selection. These remain exploratory patterns for validation;
the stage and strength notebooks retain independent-unit tests, while the parent-reversal screen
is descriptive and does not make a significance claim. Optional stricter-filter comparisons do
not decide which results enter this catalogue.

\finding{\textbf{Priority order for follow-up.}
(1) The common colon plasma-attraction and goblet-avoidance changes are large and staging-stable.
(2) The two duodenal parent--complex reversals are especially clear conditional patterns.
(3) The colon reversal and duodenal muscle--endothelial--plasma\_CD38 avoidance merit follow-up,
with the sparse control-complex and threshold-comparison caveats shown below.
(4) The Paneth--neuron and conditional-Lift patterns are more tentative.}
