Each FOV-level pair is attraction ($+1$), avoidance ($-1$), or no significant rule ($0$).
Eligibility is recalculated separately for every rule: an FOV is eligible for that rule only
when both of its cell types have at least 20 cells. Net score is
$100(N_{\rm attraction}-N_{\rm avoidance})/N_{\rm eligible}$.
Lift is observed co-occurrence divided by its independence expectation: 1 is neutral,
values above 1 support attraction, and values below 1 support avoidance.
\hypertarget{test-guide}{\textbf{How to read the tests.}}
``Ordered FDR'' asks whether values move consistently from Control to Mild to Severe.
C--M, M--S and C--S compare the named pair of stages. ``Organ FDR'' compares Colon with
Duodenum within one stage. FDR values are corrected for the relevant rule screen; smaller
is stronger, and values below 0.05 are called significant.
Temporal FDR tests the largest biopsy-level net-score gap among the three post-transplant
windows; Control is shown only as context. Pooled temporal FDR jointly corrects all rules
in two broader post-transplant comparisons.
The discovery notebook screens every pair rule and draws a result when its best corrected
test has FDR $\leq0.05$ and its largest stage gap is at least 20 percentage points; no rule
name participates in selection. The paper-alignment family screen is deliberately broader:
it draws a detailed result when any corrected test has FDR $\leq0.10$ or the
Control--Severe gap is at least 30 percentage points. Rule--abundance plots use the same
eligible FOVs for all three lines. These are spatial associations, not causal mechanisms.
}}

\section{Evidence that the method resolves spatial structure}

The clearest separation between composition and organization is Duodenal
Fibroblast $\rightarrow$ Muscle: under pathological staging the rule becomes much more
avoidant while both cell populations increase, so the blue trajectory cannot be a copy of
cell loss.

\evidence{Strong}

  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{summary_downloads/tex_method_fibroblast_muscle_path.pdf}
  \caption{\changevalues{Fibroblast $\rightarrow$ Muscle}{Fibroblast}{Muscle}
  The x-axis reports FOVs with a significant rule / eligible FOVs. Eligibility requires at
  least 20 cells of both types. See the \hyperlink{test-guide}{test guide above}.}
  \label{fig:change-values}
\end{figure}

The stage-matched FOV examples make the same result spatially concrete: the upper row keeps
the complete tissue context, while the lower row isolates Fibroblast and Muscle cells.
These maps illustrate representative rule-bearing FOVs; the preceding aggregate plot is
the evidence for the stage change.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.96\textwidth]{summary_downloads/tex_method_fibroblast_muscle_fovs.pdf}
  \caption{Within each pathological stage, the displayed FOV is selected automatically:

The same Fibroblast $\rightarrow$ Muscle separation appears when the biopsies are regrouped
by clinical score, which makes it unlikely that the result is created by one severity

\evidence{Strong}

Direction is also retained rather than collapsing a cell pair into undirected co-occurrence:
Colon CD4T $\rightarrow$ Goblet relaxes strongly with pathological severity, while the
reverse rule starts more avoidant and changes by a different amount.

\evidence{Moderate}

The method also preserves organ context. Goblet $\rightarrow$ Endothelial remains strongly

\subsection*{Metric strength compared with rule prevalence}

Prevalence and Lift expose different changes: prevalence asks how often a rule is found
among eligible FOVs, whereas Lift measures its strength only where it is found. The screens
below first rank every rule by its largest observed stage gap; rule names are not chosen in
advance. For avoidance, a smaller Lift means stronger spatial separation.

Colon SMV $\rightarrow$ Endothelial is the clearest attractive-strength result: pathological
median Lift falls from about 3.4 in Control to 2.2 in Severe disease (ordered Lift FDR 0.015),
while attractive prevalence changes much less. The method therefore detects weakening of a
persisting spatial relationship, not merely disappearance of the rule.

\evidence{Strong}

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.49\textwidth]{summary_downloads/tex_metric_colon_smv_endothelial_path_metric.pdf}
  \includegraphics[width=0.49\textwidth]{summary_downloads/tex_metric_colon_smv_endothelial_path_prevalence.pdf}
  \caption{\metricprevalencevalues{attraction}{an attractive rule} Stages use pathological
  score in Colon. Lift FDR and attraction-prevalence FDR are separate corrected tests; see
  the \hyperlink{test-guide}{test guide above}.}
  \label{fig:metric-prevalence-values}
\end{figure}

The same rule is again the largest attractive-Lift gap when Colon biopsies are regrouped by
clinical score. Its corrected evidence is weaker (best Lift FDR 0.065), but the independent
grouping preserves the loss of strength.

\evidence{Moderate}
